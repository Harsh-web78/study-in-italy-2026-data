# Study in Italy 2026/27 — Data & Source Guide

A source-backed reference for international students researching higher education in Italy for the **2026/27 academic year**.

This repository collects practical information on:

* tuition fees and living costs
* scholarships and financial support
* university applications and admissions
* Universitaly pre-enrolment
* student-visa procedures
* important 2026/27 dates
* official sources for further verification

> **Important:** Fees, deadlines, scholarship conditions and visa procedures can vary by university, programme, applicant category and jurisdiction. Always verify the latest requirements with the relevant university, Italian authorities and official portals before applying.

## 1. Italy: tuition and living-cost snapshot

The European Education Area's Italy country profile gives these general ranges:

| Item                        |    General reference |
| --------------------------- | -------------------: |
| Public university tuition   |     €900–€4,000/year |
| Private institution tuition | €6,000–€20,000+/year |
| Typical living costs        |    €700–€1,100/month |

These are country-level reference ranges, not guaranteed fees for a specific university or course.

**Official source:**
https://education.ec.europa.eu/study-in-europe/country-profiles/italy

## 2. Scholarships

Italy has multiple scholarship routes, including:

* regional scholarships
* university/institutional scholarships
* MAECI scholarships
* Erasmus+ and other mobility funding
* PhD/research funding

### MAECI 2026/27

The Italian Ministry of Foreign Affairs and International Cooperation published the 2026/27 call for scholarships for foreign students and Italian citizens living abroad.

* Application deadline: **26 March 2026**
* Total scholarship amount for eligible MAECI winners covered by the relevant provisions: **€10,800**
* Payment and eligibility conditions are governed by the official call.

Official sources:

* https://studyinitaly.esteri.it/

## 3. Universitaly and international-student procedures

For international students applying to Italian higher education, **Universitaly** is the official portal used for pre-enrolment for visa purposes.

For academic years **2026/27 and 2027/28**, the official international-student procedures state that:

* visa-related pre-enrolment is handled through Universitaly;
* the competent diplomatic/consular representation makes the final visa decision;
* university validation or pre-enrolment does **not** guarantee that a visa will be issued;
* for 2026/27, the general deadline stated for visa applications for ordinary degree programmes is **30 November 2026**, while individual institutions may set earlier course-specific dates.

Official source:

https://www.universitaly.it/it/studenti-stranieri

## 4. Typical application flow

1. Identify universities and programmes.
2. Check exact programme admission requirements.
3. Check tuition fees, scholarships and financial requirements.
4. Prepare academic and supporting documents.
5. Apply to the university according to its published procedure.
6. If required, complete Universitaly pre-enrolment.
7. Prepare the student-visa application using the requirements of the competent Italian diplomatic/consular authority.
8. Complete post-arrival residence formalities and university enrolment.

The exact sequence can differ by programme and applicant category.

## 5. Why university-level data matters

Country-level ranges are useful for orientation, but applicants should verify the **specific university and programme** because fees and admission rules can differ substantially.

Useful data points to compare include:

* annual tuition mechanism
* income-based fee bands
* application fee
* admission deadlines
* language requirements
* entrance tests
* scholarship deadlines
* document requirements
* Universitaly/visa deadlines
* programme-specific restrictions

## 6. Data quality and methodology

This repository follows a simple source hierarchy:

**Primary sources first**

* Italian government / ministry portals

* individual university websites

**Secondary sources**

* European Education Area / European Commission

Secondary sources are useful for discovery and context, but programme-specific or legal requirements should be checked against the relevant primary source.
